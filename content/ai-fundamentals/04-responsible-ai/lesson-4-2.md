## A deliberate workflow

An effective prompt tells a system what you need and supplies relevant context. State the task, audience and desired format. Add constraints such as length, tone or material that must be included. For example: “Explain overfitting in 150 words for a beginner. Use UK English, define the term and include one simple analogy.” A clear prompt improves the chance of a useful response, although it cannot guarantee accuracy.

Work iteratively. Review the first result, point out what is missing and ask focused follow-up questions. Do not include personal, confidential or commercially sensitive information unless you have permission and understand how the service handles it.

Treat output as material to evaluate, not an authority. Check important factual claims against reliable primary sources where possible. Open cited sources and confirm that they exist, say what the output claims and are suitable for the topic. Test generated calculations and code. Look for missing perspectives, outdated information, stereotypes and confident language unsupported by evidence.

In education, **academic integrity** means that submitted work honestly represents your learning and follows your institution’s rules. Policies differ. AI might be allowed for brainstorming in one assessment and prohibited in another. Ask when uncertain, keep a record of how you used the tool and never present generated work as entirely your own when that would be misleading.

**Transparency** is useful beyond education. Acknowledge material AI assistance when readers, clients or colleagues would reasonably expect to know. Remain accountable for the final result: edit it, verify it and ensure it is appropriate.

A practical routine is: define the goal, protect sensitive information, prompt clearly, inspect the output, verify important details and disclose meaningful assistance. Used this way, AI can support judgement and learning without replacing either.
