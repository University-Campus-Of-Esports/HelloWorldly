## AI around you

You may use AI many times without seeing an “AI” label. A **recommendation system** ranks music, films or products by estimating which items may be relevant to you. It can use signals such as items you selected before, similarities between items and broad patterns across many users. It predicts interest; it does not read your mind.

A search engine also ranks possibilities. When you enter a query, systems interpret the words and order pages that are likely to help. Navigation applications predict journey times from maps, traffic observations and past travel patterns. As conditions change, the predicted route can change too.

**Image recognition** systems identify patterns in pixels. A phone might group photographs containing similar faces, while an accessibility tool might describe objects in a scene. These predictions can be wrong, particularly when an image differs from the examples used to develop the system.

Video games use several forms of AI. A character may follow hand-written behaviour rules, search for a route through a level or adapt its actions. Not every game character learns while you play; “game AI” often combines conventional algorithms and rules to create believable behaviour.

**Generative AI** creates new content, such as text, images, audio or computer code. A text generator predicts a plausible continuation from the prompt and its learned patterns. The result can sound confident without being accurate.

These applications have different goals, but prediction connects many of them. One predicts a useful page, another a traffic delay and another the next part of an image or sentence. Knowing the goal helps you question whether an output is suitable and what might happen when the prediction fails.
